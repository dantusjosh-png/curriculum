# Persuasion and Human Psychology

**Status:** Not started · **Est. length:** 7 modules ≈ 14 sessions

Your sales background, your content, and your journalism all run on this. The course covers how people actually decide (not how they think they decide), the load-bearing cognitive biases, the mechanics of influence, and — critically — defense: knowing the techniques is how you avoid being played by them.

## Syllabus

| # | Module | Core question | Status |
|:--|:-------|:--------------|:-------|
| 1 | How People Actually Decide | Dual-process thinking, heuristics, why "irrational" is often adaptive | Not started |
| 2 | The Load-Bearing Biases | The dozen biases that explain most real-world behavior | Not started |
| 3 | Influence Mechanics | Reciprocity, social proof, scarcity, authority — why the classics work | Not started |
| 4 | Framing & Anchoring | How presentation changes decisions without changing facts | Not started |
| 5 | Narrative as Technology | Why stories persuade when arguments don't | Not started |
| 6 | Applied Persuasion | Sales, copywriting, negotiation — the ethical toolkit | Not started |
| 7 | Defense Against the Dark Arts | Manipulation recognition, propaganda techniques, inoculation | Not started |

**Capstone:** deconstruct one masterful persuasive artifact (a sales page, speech, or viral piece) — name every technique operating in it — then build your own persuasive piece for a real goal of yours, with an annotated version showing your choices.

## Positioning

Assumes: practical sales/content experience. Does not assume: psychology coursework. Bias: mechanisms you can use and spot in the wild; replication-crisis-aware (we skip the pop-psych findings that didn't hold up).
