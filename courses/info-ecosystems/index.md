# How Information Ecosystems Work

**Status:** Not started · **Est. length:** 7 modules ≈ 14 sessions

You are both a producer and a subject of these systems. This course covers how information actually flows in 2026: the epistemics of knowing what's true, the business models that shape what you see, how recommendation algorithms rank reality, and how bad information wins. Offense (better content, better journalism) and defense (a harder-to-fool mind) at once.

## Syllabus

| # | Module | Core question | Status |
|:--|:-------|:--------------|:-------|
| 1 | Epistemics | How do you actually know what's true? Evidence, calibration, updating | Not started |
| 2 | The Attention Economy | Who pays for what you read, and what that does to what gets written | Not started |
| 3 | Recommendation Algorithms | How feeds actually rank — engagement objectives and their side effects | Not started |
| 4 | Misinformation Dynamics | Virality mechanics, information laundering, manufactured consensus | Not started |
| 5 | Verification in Practice | OSINT basics, sourcing hygiene, how professionals check claims fast | Not started |
| 6 | The Producer's Dilemma | Audience-building without becoming what the algorithm wants | Not started |
| 7 | Synthesis: Auditing the Ecosystem | Tracing how one claim moved through the system | Not started |

**Capstone:** trace a real information cascade — pick one viral claim from your feeds, reconstruct its full path (origin → amplification → mutation → your screen), and write it up. Plus a documented audit of your own information diet.

## Positioning

Assumes: heavy platform fluency as a creator/consumer. Does not assume: media studies background. Bias: mechanisms and incentives over hand-wringing; everything grounded in systems you use daily.
