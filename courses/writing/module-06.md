# Module 6: Rhetoric: The Figures

**Stage II — Voice & Style** · **Status:** Not started

**Core question:** Why do certain sentences lodge in memory for centuries?

**What you walk away with:** Command of the named devices — anaphora, chiasmus, tricolon, asyndeton — behind nearly every memorable line in English.

## Watch & Read

The anchor sources for this module. Understanding checks and written questions are added when the module opens.

**Read: [Silva Rhetoricae — the Forest of Rhetoric (BYU)](http://rhetoric.byu.edu/)**

> ~20 min browsing. The free scholarly encyclopedia of rhetorical figures. Focus on the figures of repetition and the figures of arrangement.

**Study: [Martin Luther King Jr., 'I Have a Dream' — text and audio, American Rhetoric](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/speeches/mlkihaveadream.htm)**

> ~17 min. Ranked the greatest American speech of the twentieth century. Read the text while listening to the delivery, and mark every figure you can name. The 'I have a dream' section was improvised — which is only possible because the devices were already in his body.

**Browse: [American Rhetoric: Top 100 Speeches](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/top100speechesall.html)**

> Audio and full text for the hundred most consequential American speeches. You'll return here in Module 13.
