# Module 10: Narrative & Scene

**Stage III — Forms That Pay** · **Status:** Not started

**Core question:** How do you put a reader inside a room they were never in?

**What you walk away with:** Scene construction: the reporting that makes it possible and the selection that makes it land.

---

## Lecture (framing)

_Delivered when you reach this module — the professor's briefing on what these sources are doing and what to watch for._

## Watch & Read

The anchor sources for this module. Understanding checks and written questions are added when the module opens.

**Read: [Frank Sinatra Has a Cold — Gay Talese](https://archive.org/details/franksinatrahasc0000tale)**

> ~60 min (split across two sessions). Often called the greatest magazine piece ever published. Talese never got an interview with Sinatra — the entire profile is built from observation, which is precisely what makes it a masterclass. Watch how much characterization rides on physical detail.

**Listen: [Talese reads from the piece — Esquire Classic audio](https://soundcloud.com/esquireclassics/esquire-classic-frank-sinatra-has-a-cold-by-gay-talese)**

> Optional, free. Hearing it read changes what you notice.

**Study: [Nieman Storyboard annotations](https://niemanstoryboard.org/)**

> ~20 min. Great stories annotated line by line by the journalists who wrote them, explaining every decision. The closest thing to watching a professional think.


## Discussion

_Socratic questions, posed when the module opens._

## Application

Report and write one 500-word scene from your own life or beat — a trading floor, a meeting, a room you were in — built only from observed detail. No interior monologue, no summary.
