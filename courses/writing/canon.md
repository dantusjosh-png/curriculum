# The Canon

**The writers you'll learn from, and precisely what to steal from each.**

---

This is not a list of great books. It's a list of *techniques attached to the people who executed them best*, because that's how imitation works: you don't steal a writer wholesale, you steal one move. Every writer here is assigned somewhere in the course, and every entry names the specific thing to take.

## The prose stylists

**George Orwell** — *the moral case for plain English.*
Steal: the concrete noun where an abstraction was expected; the refusal of prefabricated phrases. Read: [Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/), [Why I Write](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/why-i-write/), [Shooting an Elephant](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/shooting-an-elephant/). All free at The Orwell Foundation.

**Ernest Hemingway** — *omission as a source of power.*
Steal: the iceberg — what you leave out strengthens what remains; declarative sentences that refuse to editorialize. Read: [The Art of Fiction No. 21](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/4825/the-art-of-fiction-no-21-ernest-hemingway), *Paris Review*.

**Joan Didion** — *syntax as meaning; the cold eye.*
Steal: the sentence that withholds judgment while arranging facts so the judgment is inevitable; her use of repetition and the long catalogue. Read: [The Art of Nonfiction No. 1](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/5601/the-art-of-nonfiction-no-1-joan-didion), and [Why I Write](https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/10/16/why-i-write-joan-didion/).

**James Baldwin** — *rage held at precision temperature.*
Steal: the long sentence that accumulates moral weight without losing control; direct address to the reader. Read: [The Art of Fiction No. 78](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/2994/the-art-of-fiction-no-78-james-baldwin), [My Dungeon Shook: A Letter to My Nephew](https://www.historyisaweapon.com/defcon1/baldwindungeonshook.html).

**Kurt Vonnegut** — *plainness as a moral stance; comic timing.*
Steal: the very short paragraph as a punch; refusing to sound smarter than your reader. Read: [How to Write With Style](https://www.openculture.com/2014/11/kurt-vonnegut-explains-how-to-write-with-style.html), and watch [The Shapes of Stories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oP3c1h8v2ZQ).

## The reporters

**Gay Talese** — *scene built entirely from observation.*
Steal: characterization through physical detail; the profile written without the subject's cooperation. Read: [Frank Sinatra Has a Cold](https://archive.org/details/franksinatrahasc0000tale).

**John McPhee** — *structure as the primary craft.*
Steal: designing the architecture before drafting; the deliberate placement of the digression. Read: [The Art of Nonfiction No. 3](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/6774/the-art-of-nonfiction-no-3-john-mcphee), and [how he assembles pieces](https://www.princeton.edu/news/2007/05/07/assembling-written-word-mcphee-reveals-how-pieces-go-together).

**Michael Lewis** — *finance made narrative.*
Steal: the outsider-protagonist as a device for explaining a system; complex mechanics delivered through character. Closest model in the canon to what you're trying to build on your own beat.

## The teachers

**William Zinsser** — clarity, simplicity, brevity, humanity. Read: [Writing English as a Second Language](https://theamericanscholar.org/writing-english-as-a-second-language/).

**Verlyn Klinkenborg** — the sentence as the unit; unlearning school. Read: [on writing more clearly](https://lithub.com/verlyn-klinkenborg-on-writing-more-clearly/).

**Anne Lamott** — permission to write badly first. Read: [Shitty First Drafts](https://wrd.as.uky.edu/sites/default/files/1-Shitty%20First%20Drafts.pdf).

**George Saunders** — revision as hundreds of micro-adjustments. Read: [What Writers Really Do When They Write](https://longreads.com/2017/03/16/george-saunders-what-writers-really-do-when-they-write/).

**Roy Peter Clark** — the toolkit. Read: [Fifty Writing Tools](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2006/fifty-writing-tools-quick-list/).

**Steven Pinker** — why experts write badly. Watch: [The Sense of Style](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3ZKTmsgqi0U).

## The orators

**Martin Luther King Jr.** — [I Have a Dream](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/speeches/mlkihaveadream.htm): anaphora, and the improvised passage that only works because the devices were already in his body.

**Winston Churchill** — [We Shall Fight on the Beaches](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/speeches/winstonchurchillweshallfightonthebeaches.htm): Anglo-Saxon monosyllables under maximum pressure.

**Abraham Lincoln** — the Gettysburg Address: 272 words; parallel structure; the tricolon that closes it.

Browse the full [Top 100 Speeches](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/top100speechesall.html) archive — text and audio, free.

## The vault

**[The Paris Review Interviews](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews)** — over four hundred long-form craft interviews, free to read: Hemingway, Didion, Baldwin, McPhee, Vonnegut, Morrison, Nabokov, García Márquez. If you exhaust this course and want more, this is the graduate program. Read them the way a fighter watches tape.
