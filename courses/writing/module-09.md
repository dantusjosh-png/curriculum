# Module 9: Structure & Architecture

**Stage III — Forms That Pay** · **Status:** Not started

**Core question:** How do you design a piece before you write it?

**What you walk away with:** The ability to choose a structure deliberately instead of discovering one by accident on draft five.

## Watch & Read

The anchor sources for this module. Understanding checks and written questions are added when the module opens.

**Read: [Assembling the written word: McPhee reveals how the pieces go together](https://www.princeton.edu/news/2007/05/07/assembling-written-word-mcphee-reveals-how-pieces-go-together)**

> ~10 min. McPhee is the most structurally obsessive nonfiction writer alive — he diagrams pieces before drafting. This free account covers the method his New Yorker essay 'Structure' lays out at length.

**Read: [John McPhee on structure — annotated notes](https://lincolnmullen.com/blog/john-mcphee-on-structure-and-his-text-editor/)**

> ~8 min. A close reading of McPhee's structural principles with the diagrams described.

**Watch: [Kurt Vonnegut on the Shapes of Stories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oP3c1h8v2ZQ)**

> ~4 min. Vonnegut graphs narrative arcs on a whiteboard: man in hole, boy meets girl, Cinderella. Funny, and a genuinely usable analytic tool.
