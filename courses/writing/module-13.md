# Module 13: Writing for the Ear

**Stage IV — The Professional** · **Status:** Not started

**Core question:** How does prose change when it must be heard rather than read?

**What you walk away with:** Speaking well, and writing prose whose rhythm survives being read aloud.

---

## Lecture (framing)

_Delivered when you reach this module — the professor's briefing on what these sources are doing and what to watch for._

## Watch & Read

The anchor sources for this module. Understanding checks and written questions are added when the module opens.

**Study: [American Rhetoric: Top 100 Speeches — audio and text](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/top100speechesall.html)**

> ~40 min. Pick three from the top twenty. Read each text first, then listen. Mark every place the written version under-predicts the spoken effect — that gap is everything this module is about.

**Study: [Churchill, 'We Shall Fight on the Beaches' — text and audio](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/speeches/winstonchurchillweshallfightonthebeaches.htm)**

> ~10 min. Note the Anglo-Saxon monosyllables in the famous passage — Zinsser's point from Module 1, deployed under maximum pressure.


## Discussion

_Socratic questions, posed when the module opens._

## Application

Record yourself reading your Milestone 3 piece aloud. Mark every place you stumbled — those are prose defects, not reading errors. Revise, then deliver a 3-minute spoken version of the same story.
