# Module 7: Imitation & Pastiche

**Stage II — Voice & Style** · **Status:** Not started

**Core question:** How do you steal from the best without becoming a copy?

**What you walk away with:** Deliberate imitation as a training method — the oldest and most reliable route to a voice of your own.

## Watch & Read

The anchor sources for this module. Understanding checks and written questions are added when the module opens.

**Read: [Benjamin Franklin's Autobiography — the Spectator method](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20203/20203-h/20203-h.htm)**

> Read the section on how he taught himself to write: he took essays from *The Spectator*, made notes, waited until he'd forgotten the wording, rewrote them from his notes, then compared his version to the original and studied every place he came up short. Hunter S. Thompson later typed out *The Great Gatsby* in full for the same reason.

**Read: [Joan Didion, The Art of Nonfiction No. 1 — The Paris Review](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/5601/the-art-of-nonfiction-no-1-joan-didion)**

> ~30 min. Didion typed out Hemingway's sentences to learn how they worked. Watch for what she says about learning structure by retyping other people's paragraphs.

## Application

The Franklin drill, run properly: take 300 words from a writer in [The Canon](canon), reduce to notes, wait 48 hours, rewrite from notes, then compare line by line and write up every place you fell short.
