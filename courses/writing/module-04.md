# Module 4: Revision

**Stage I — The Sentence** · **Status:** Not started

**Core question:** How does a bad first draft become a good final one?

**What you walk away with:** A repeatable revision process, and the psychological tolerance to produce bad drafts on purpose.

## Watch & Read

The anchor sources for this module. Understanding checks and written questions are added when the module opens.

**Read: [Shitty First Drafts — Anne Lamott](https://wrd.as.uky.edu/sites/default/files/1-Shitty%20First%20Drafts.pdf)**

> ~10 min, PDF. From *Bird by Bird*, the most assigned essay on writing in America. Her permission structure — that professionals produce garbage first drafts and this is the process working, not failing — is the thing most amateurs never learn.

**Read: [What Writers Really Do When They Write — George Saunders](https://longreads.com/2017/03/16/george-saunders-what-writers-really-do-when-they-write/)**

> ~15 min. Saunders won the Booker Prize and teaches at Syracuse. His revision method is almost mechanical: read your own draft as a stranger, mark your reaction on a meter, adjust, repeat hundreds of times. Watch for what he means by 'revising your reader up.'

**Watch: [The Gap — Ira Glass](https://vimeo.com/85040589)**

> ~2 min. The most useful two minutes about creative work ever recorded: why your taste exceeds your ability at the start, why that gap is normal, and why volume is the only cure.
