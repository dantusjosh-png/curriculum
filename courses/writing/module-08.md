# Module 8: Developing Your Own Style

**Stage II — Voice & Style** · **Status:** Not started

**Core question:** What is your voice, and can you describe it precisely enough to write in it on demand?

**What you walk away with:** A named, reproducible voice — the thing that makes an editor recognize your work without the byline.

_Delivered when you reach this module — the professor's briefing on what these sources are doing and what to watch for._

## Watch & Read

The anchor sources for this module. Understanding checks and written questions are added when the module opens.

**Read: [Why I Write — George Orwell](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/why-i-write/)**

> ~15 min. Orwell names his four motives for writing and admits which one drives him. Do the same honestly for yourself; it determines what you should be writing.

**Read: [Why I Write — Joan Didion (via The Marginalian)](https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/10/16/why-i-write-joan-didion/)**

> ~10 min. Didion stole her title from Orwell and made the argument physical: writing as an act of imposing yourself on other people. Watch for the pictures-in-the-mind passage.

**Read: [James Baldwin, The Art of Fiction No. 78 — The Paris Review](https://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/2994/the-art-of-fiction-no-78-james-baldwin)**

> ~40 min. Baldwin on finding a voice that could hold both rage and precision — the hardest technical problem in the language, solved.

## Application

**MILESTONE 2** — one story told four times: three times in borrowed voices (from writers in The Canon), once in your own. Plus a written specification of your voice: sentence habits, register, characteristic moves, what you refuse to do.
