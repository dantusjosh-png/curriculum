# The Lexicon

**Your vocabulary system and commonplace book.** Not a module — a practice that runs the length of the course.

---

## Why this page exists

You recognize roughly five to ten times more words than you use. Reading grows the first number automatically. Only deliberate practice grows the second, and only the second one appears in your prose. Everything on this page exists to move words across that border.

The method is old. Renaissance humanists kept **commonplace books** — notebooks of other people's sentences, organized for reuse. Milton kept one. Locke published a system for keeping one. Emerson, Woolf, Didion, and most working writers since have kept some version. It survives because it works, and because the alternative — memorizing definitions — reliably produces writers who misuse impressive words.

## The three-step method

**1. Capture — always with the sentence.**
When you meet a word or phrase you *recognize but wouldn't have produced yourself*, record it inside the full sentence you found it in. Never the bare word. The sentence carries the register, the connotation, and the grammatical company the word keeps, and those are the things a definition can't give you.

**2. Interrogate.**
For each entry, note three things:
- **Etymology** ([etymonline.com](https://www.etymonline.com/)) — the story that makes it stick.
- **Register** — where does this word live? Legal, clinical, literary, street, technical? Using a word in the wrong register is the most common way an expanded vocabulary backfires.
- **The near-synonym it is *not*.** *Reticent* is not *reluctant*. *Fulsome* is not *full*. *Enormity* is not *enormousness*. Writing the distinction down is what installs it.

**3. Deploy — three uses in two weeks.**
A captured word is not yours until you have used it in your own writing three times. Force it: work each new word into a Substack post, an email, a course exercise. Some will feel wrong in your mouth — that's information too. Discard those honestly rather than wearing them like a borrowed jacket.

## Standing rules

- **Ten entries minimum between modules.** Non-negotiable; it's part of module completion.
- **No word you can't imagine yourself saying out loud.** A vocabulary that exists only to impress will make your prose worse, not better. Orwell's whole argument, applied to you.
- **Capture phrases and constructions too**, not only words. A verb doing unexpected work, a rhythm, an unusual preposition — those are often more valuable than rare nouns.
- **Steal from your reading in this course.** Orwell, Didion, Baldwin, Talese, and Hemingway are the assigned suppliers.
