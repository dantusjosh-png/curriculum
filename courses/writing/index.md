# Writing and Reporting Craft

**Status:** Not started · **Est. length:** 8 modules ≈ 16 sessions (+ ongoing reps)

Your stated primary goal. This is a craft course, not a knowledge course: every module ends with words on a page, and the feedback loop matters more than the lecture. We study the mechanics of clear prose, the shapes stories take, how professionals actually report, and the legal lines you must not cross the moment you publish.

**Note on structure:** unlike the other courses, this one is designed to run *continuously* — its exercises can piggyback on whatever else you're learning (e.g., write the Markets capstone using these tools).

## Syllabus

| # | Module | Core question | Status |
|:--|:-------|:--------------|:-------|
| 1 | The Sentence & the Paragraph | Clarity, economy, rhythm — the mechanics under all good prose | Not started |
| 2 | Narrative Structure | Ledes, nut grafs, story shapes, tension and payoff | Not started |
| 3 | Argument Construction | Claims, evidence, steelmanning — writing that persuades honestly | Not started |
| 4 | The Reporting Process | Finding stories, developing sources, documents and data | Not started |
| 5 | Interviewing | Preparation, question design, listening, getting usable quotes | Not started |
| 6 | Editing & Revision | Self-editing discipline, fact-checking, killing your darlings | Not started |
| 7 | Media Law & Ethics | Libel, fair use, privacy, corrections — what matters the moment you hit publish | Not started |
| 8 | Voice & Beat | Developing a distinctive voice and owning a beat (yours: markets) | Not started |

**Capstone:** one fully reported, edited, legally-vetted feature on your beat, published — with at least one real interview and one document/data source.

## Positioning

Assumes: you write regularly (content, posts). Does not assume: newsroom experience. Bias: reps + feedback over theory; every session produces or improves actual writing.
