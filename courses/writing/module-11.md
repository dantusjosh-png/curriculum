# Module 11: The Essay & Argument

**Stage III — Forms That Pay** · **Status:** Not started

**Core question:** How do you make a case that survives a hostile reader?

**What you walk away with:** Argument architecture: claims, evidence, concession, and the personal essay's harder trick of arguing through experience.

## Watch & Read

The anchor sources for this module. Understanding checks and written questions are added when the module opens.

**Read: [Shooting an Elephant — George Orwell](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/shooting-an-elephant/)**

> ~20 min. The model for the essay that argues by narrating. Orwell never states his thesis about empire as a proposition — he makes you feel it, then names it once.

**Read: [James Baldwin, 'A Letter to My Nephew' — The Progressive](https://progressive.org/magazine/letter-my-nephew/)**

> ~15 min. Published 1962, free. Argument as address: how the form of a letter permits claims an essay couldn't carry.

**Read: [Michel de Montaigne, selected Essays (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3600/3600-h/3600-h.htm)**

> ~25 min, sample two or three. The man who invented the form in the 1570s. Watch how he thinks *on the page* rather than reporting conclusions reached elsewhere.

## Application

A 900-word argumentative essay on a contested question in your field, containing a genuine concession — a point where you give real ground to the other side and are stronger for it.
