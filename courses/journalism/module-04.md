# Module 4: Structure & the Pitch

**Status:** Not started

**Core question:** Getting the story built — inverted pyramid vs. narrative
structure, ledes, and nut grafs — and getting it bought: pitching an editor
who's never heard of you.

**Anchor resources (verified live):**
- [The Nut Graf, Part I](https://www.poynter.org/archive/2003/the-nut-graf-part-i/) — Poynter, 2003. What a nut graf is (the paragraph that tells the reader why the story matters and why now), where it emerged from (Barney Kilgore's Wall Street Journal, 1941), and the rules for writing one (don't reveal the ending, keep it to a few sentences, no editorializing, anticipate the reader's question).
- [How to Pitch Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/pitch-freelance-media-news/) — this is literally one real editor's actual submission guidelines, not a generic listicle, which is the point: read it as a case study in what any editor wants (answer "why now / so what," show your reporting plan, match the outlet's tone, disclose conflicts, pitch the idea before the draft) and generalize from a real example rather than an abstraction.

**Capstone for this course:** write and actually send one real pitch to one
real outlet.
